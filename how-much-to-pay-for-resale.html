How much should you pay for an item to resell?

Use a maximum-buy-price formula to work backward from your expected sale price, marketplace fees, operating costs, and target profit.

Open the free reseller profit calculator →

By FlipMargin Editorial · Updated November 5, 2026 · Practical guide for US resellers

Set a target before negotiating a purchase

A common resale mistake is buying an item because the gap between its purchase price and expected sale price looks large. That gap is not profit. Marketplace fees, shipping, packaging, promotion, and other costs all come out before you know what remains.

Instead of asking only, “What could I sell this for?”, ask: “What is the most I can pay and still hit my profit target?” This turns a resale opportunity into a repeatable decision.

Use this maximum-buy-price formula

Start with the expected revenue: item sale price plus any shipping charged to the buyer. Then subtract estimated percentage fees, fixed fees, your own shipping and packaging costs, other costs, and the profit you want to keep.

Maximum purchase price = (sale price + buyer-paid shipping) × (1 − total percentage fee rate) − fixed fees − seller-paid shipping − packaging − other costs − target profit

If you expect a 13.6% marketplace fee and a 2% promotion fee, your total percentage fee rate in this simplified formula is 15.6%. Do not include a promotion rate unless it is likely to apply to the sale.

Fee-base caveat: This formula assumes the percentage fees are modeled on item price plus buyer-paid shipping. Actual marketplace fee bases can also include tax or other amounts. Add a buffer or verify the exact category and transaction rules before committing cash.

Example: what is the most you should pay?

Suppose you expect an item to sell for $75 and the buyer will pay $6 shipping. Your estimated costs are $8 for the shipping label, $1.50 for packaging, and $2 for other costs. You want at least $20 profit. Assume the standard simplified non-Store eBay rate of 13.6% for most categories and a $0.40 per-order fee.

  • Modeled revenue: $75 + $6 = $81.00
  • Percentage fee: $81 × 13.6% = $11.02
  • Fixed per-order fee: $0.40
  • Seller-paid shipping, packaging, and other costs: $8 + $1.50 + $2 = $11.50
  • Target profit: $20.00

Maximum purchase price = $81 − $11.02 − $0.40 − $11.50 − $20 = $38.08.

In this scenario, paying more than about $38.08 would push estimated profit below your $20 target, before any omitted fees, taxes, returns, or unexpected costs. A safer offer may be lower to leave room for uncertainty.

Choose a profit target that fits the risk

There is no universal profit target for every item. A fast-selling, easy-to-ship item may justify a smaller absolute profit than a fragile, bulky, seasonal, or slow-moving item that ties up cash. Consider:

  • Sell-through: how likely the item is to sell within your expected timeframe.
  • Condition risk: missing parts, hidden defects, testing time, and return likelihood.
  • Shipping complexity: dimensions, weight, breakability, and insurance.
  • Cash tied up: how long your money may be unavailable for other opportunities.
  • Price volatility: whether current sold prices are stable or falling.
  • Platform fit: category fees, seller plan, ad costs, and buyer expectations.

Use margin and ROI together

Margin and ROI answer different questions. Profit margin is profit divided by sale revenue. ROI is profit divided by the cash invested in the item and its related costs. A low-cost item can have a high ROI but a small dollar profit; an expensive item can have a larger dollar profit but a lower ROI.

Profit margin = profit ÷ sale revenue × 100
ROI = profit ÷ invested costs × 100

Use margin to understand how much of each sale dollar remains. Use ROI to compare how effectively your buying budget is working. Also consider how long the item may take to sell—an annualized return is different from a simple per-item ROI.

Build a repeatable buying checklist

  1. Check completed/sold listings for a realistic expected sale price.
  2. Estimate buyer-paid shipping and your actual shipping label cost separately.
  3. Select the marketplace and fee preset that matches the intended sale.
  4. Enter packaging, promotion, listing fees, and other likely costs.
  5. Choose a target profit in dollars or as a share of revenue.
  6. Calculate the maximum purchase price and offer below it when uncertainty is high.
  7. Walk away if the numbers only work under an optimistic sale price or perfect condition.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use profit margin or ROI to decide what to pay?

Use both. Margin measures profit relative to sale revenue, while ROI measures profit relative to invested costs. Neither alone captures how long inventory may take to sell or the risk of a return.

Should I include buyer-paid shipping as revenue?

Yes, when estimating the order proceeds and marketplace fee base. Then subtract the shipping label or carrier cost you pay. Avoid counting buyer-paid shipping as pure profit.

How much buffer should I leave?

There is no single correct buffer. Increase it when condition, shipping, fee category, sale price, or time-to-sell is uncertain. The worked example is an estimate, not a guarantee.
