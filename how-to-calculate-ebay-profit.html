How to calculate eBay profit before buying inventory

A practical, numbers-first guide to estimating what you could keep from an eBay sale—before you spend money on inventory.

Open the free reseller profit calculator →

By FlipMargin Editorial · Updated November 5, 2026 · Practical guide for US resellers

Start with the money you actually keep

Resale profit is not simply the sale price minus the amount you paid. To estimate what an eBay sale leaves behind, count the buyer's payment, marketplace fees, postage you pay, packaging, promotion, and the item's acquisition cost. Do this before buying inventory—not after a sale has already happened.

A useful first estimate is:

Estimated profit = item price + buyer-paid shipping − purchase cost − seller-paid shipping − packaging − selling fees − other costs

Buyer-paid shipping is revenue in the calculation, but it is not free money: subtract the actual label or carrier cost separately.

Understand the eBay fee estimate

For most US categories in the standard non-Store scenario, eBay's standard final value fee is 13.6% on the total sale amount up to $7,500 per item, with 2.35% on the portion above that. The per-order fee is $0.30 for orders of $10 or less and $0.40 for orders over $10. eBay's total-sale amount can include item price, buyer-paid shipping, handling, sales tax, and other applicable amounts. Some shipping methods and categories have special rules.

FlipMargin's eBay preset is deliberately simpler: it uses 13.6% plus the appropriate $0.30/$0.40 fixed fee and calculates the percentage on item price plus buyer-paid shipping. It excludes sales tax and does not automatically model the $7,500 tier, category exceptions, Store subscription rates, below-standard seller surcharges, or optional listing and promotion charges. Treat the result as a planning estimate, not an exact eBay invoice.

Check eBay's official seller fee overview and its fee tables for your category and account.

Work through a realistic example

Imagine you buy an item for $30, expect to sell it for $80, charge the buyer $8 shipping, pay $7 for the label, and spend $1.50 on packaging. Using the simplified 13.6% eBay preset for most categories without a Store:

  • Order revenue modeled by FlipMargin: $80 + $8 = $88.00
  • Estimated percentage fee: $88 × 13.6% = $11.97
  • Per-order fee: $0.40
  • Purchase, label, and packaging: $30 + $7 + $1.50 = $38.50
  • Estimated profit before any unmodeled costs or tax effects: $88 − $11.97 − $0.40 − $38.50 = $37.13

This example assumes the standard preset applies and excludes sales tax from the modeled fee base, just as the current calculator does. The actual eBay fee can be higher if taxable amounts are included in the fee base or a category/account exception applies.

Calculate margin and return on investment

Profit margin compares estimated profit with the revenue from the sale:

Profit margin = estimated profit ÷ sale revenue × 100

ROI compares estimated profit with the money invested in acquiring and preparing the item. FlipMargin's ROI denominator includes purchase cost, seller-paid shipping, packaging, listing fee, and other entered costs. It does not use revenue as the denominator, and the displayed ROI is not the same thing as profit margin.

Use both: margin tells you what share of revenue remains, while ROI helps compare how efficiently your cash is being used across different buys.

Costs resellers often forget

  • Shipping adjustments: dimensional weight, insurance, signature confirmation, or a carrier price change.
  • Returns and refunds: return postage, damaged inventory, and fees that are not fully credited.
  • Promotion: Promoted Listings fees when a sale is attributed to an ad.
  • Listing costs: insertion fees after the free monthly allowance, optional listing upgrades, or store costs.
  • Supplies and overhead: tape, labels, printer supplies, storage, mileage, and payment or bookkeeping services.
  • Taxes: marketplace-collected sales tax is not your revenue, but it can affect the marketplace's fee calculation. Income taxes are a separate consideration.

A pre-purchase checklist

  1. Look up realistic sold prices, not just active asking prices.
  2. Estimate the total order amount, including shipping charged to the buyer.
  3. Choose the correct eBay category and confirm your seller-plan fee schedule.
  4. Estimate the shipping label and packaging before deciding what to pay.
  5. Include promotion, returns, and any other costs likely for the item.
  6. Run the estimate and decide whether the expected profit and ROI justify the risk.

Frequently asked questions

Does eBay charge a final value fee on shipping?

Generally, the fee base includes shipping charged to the buyer, with documented exceptions for certain shipping options and transactions. Check the current fee table for the exact rule.

Is 13.6% every seller's eBay rate?

No. It is the standard rate for most categories in the simplified non-Store scenario. Category, Store subscription, seller performance, and transaction details can change the fee.

Why might FlipMargin's estimate differ from my eBay payout?

The current preset excludes sales tax from its fee base and does not model every category, seller-plan, promotion, shipping, refund, or account-specific rule. Compare the estimate with the fee breakdown in eBay Seller Hub.
